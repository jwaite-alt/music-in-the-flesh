description: |-
  John Keats once described the power of the 'chameleon poet' to be 'continually in for - and filling - some other body'. In his own short life and extraordinary body of work we find him embodying men, women, nightingales, sparrows and stars, and even a bassoon. His letters and poems offer visceral accounts of physical responses to art, from the erotic encounter with the Grecian Urn to the heart-aching influence of the nightingale's song, from the 'intensity' of viewing a painting to the time Keats licked a book of poetry. 
  Join musicologist Professor Bettina Varwig and writer on Romantic poetry Dr Corinna Russell for a conversation about reading the life of the body back into our experience of music and poetry. This conversation is hosted by the Cambridge Festival as a companion event to the performance of Jeremy Thurlow's Chameleon Poet, a musical portrait of Keats for bassoon and string quartet.
